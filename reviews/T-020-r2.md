  node, which holds no char) is never a partner: in `fn f() { g(1; }` the `(` at 10 gives
  `null`." True.
  - `syntax.tree` gives `errors: [{kind: missing, start: 12, end: 12}]`, and its sexp holds
    `arguments:(arguments@10..12 (integer_literal@11..12) (MISSING ")"@12..12))`.
  - `match-bracket` 10 → `{"bracket":null}`; 12 (the `;`) → null. The real pairs: 4 and 5 →

  - It is not wider than the behaviour: the M17 binary in round 1 showed that only the MISSING
    exclusion makes 10 null.
- **`syntax.match-bracket` description:** "… has a partner, return the offsets …; otherwise
  `bracket` is `null`. Partners are bracket leaves of the same kind that are direct children
  of the same syntax node, paired left to right by a stack; a bracket the parser inserted is
  never a partner (rules in `docs/protocol/syntax.md`)." True.
  - Same kind: `( ]` → 0, 2 and 3 all give null.
  - Inserted bracket: as above.
  - Stack: the round-1 D-104 cases still pass in `bracket_worked_examples`.
  - "Bracket leaves" is qualified by the first clause ("outside strings, char literals and
    comments"), so it is not wider than the behaviour.
- **Time, first sentence:** "No time bound exists for any `syntax.*` command: the size limit
  bounds the length of the text a command accepts, not the time a parse takes or the memory it
  needs." This is D-110 r1's wording verbatim. It is consistent with the binary: a text at the
  limit is accepted (AC-8, unchanged), and memory at the limit varies about threefold with
  content (below). It claims no bound.
- **Memory section.**
