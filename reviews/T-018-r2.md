| 1 | defect | `docs/protocol/eol.md:53-54` | The exception says a position directly between a lone CR and a following `\r` "ends just before the lone CR's new LF, at the end of the line before". That is true only when the CR before it is the first of its run. Inside a run of three or more `\r`, the snap cascades: the position ends before the new LF of the *first* `\r` of the run of consecutive `\r`s, so it can move up several lines. This covers 142 of the 547 exception cases. You could also add `x\r\r\r\ry` (caret 4 → 1) as a second example. The behaviour stays, per x2. | Exhaustive check above; `kiln-cli run`: `x\r\r\r\ry`, `cursor.set 4`, `eol.normalize {to:"lf"}`, `cursor.get` → head 1, line 0 |
